Pick one

Six marks, six arguments

Each one is shown at 16 px beside it. If a mark stops meaning anything at 16 px it cannot be your favicon, your WhatsApp avatar or your app icon — and those are where merchants meet you first.

Canopy

Leading

The whole market stall: pitched roof, scalloped valance, posts, counter. A merchant who reads nothing still sees a shop open for business.

Trade-off: the most parts of the six. Posts and counter box drop away below 24 px, so the small cut is a separate drawing.

Ledger

The three bars already shipped, given rhythm and a gold “this one is paid” line. Nothing on the site has to change on day one.

Trade-off: abstract. It is a nice shape, but it does not say market, money or shop to anyone.

Cowrie

West Africa’s original money. Drawn from photographs of real Monetaria moneta — the dense toothed aperture is the feature Ghanaians actually recognise.

Trade-off: heritage can read as “traditional”, and the teeth need a separate simplified cut below 32 px.

Shutter

The roll-up shop shutter, half raised. The most literal “I am open” signal in any Ghanaian street.

Trade-off: weakest of the six at 16 px — the slats collapse into a stripe. See it beside the others.

Tray

The head-carried tray, loaded. The warmest and most human of the six — it pictures the seller, not the software.

Trade-off: at small sizes the goods drop away and it becomes a generic bowl.

Kente Diamond

Built on Adinkra construction logic — equal stroke, full symmetry, a filled counterform. Inspired by, not copied from, any single symbol.

Trade-off: reads as pattern before it reads as a mark, and pattern is easy for a competitor to echo.
