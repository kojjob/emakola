Motion — the loops

Four states, one mark

A spinning circle tells a merchant nothing. The same stall doing four different things tells them which of four things is happening — and they never have to read a status message to find out.

Loading · 1.25 s

The awning ripples

Replaces every spinner in the app. Six scallops on an 80 ms stagger — the stall is up, the cloth is moving, something is happening.

Waiting for payment · 2.1 s

A coin drops in

The coin is drawn behind the roof, so it falls in through the front of the stall and settles on the counter. Money going into the shop, no words.

Paid · 2.4 s, plays once

Counter turns green

The one place the mark is allowed to leave its palette — green is the money colour everywhere else in the kit, so it has to be here too.

Splash · 3 s

It breathes

4.5% scale, nothing else. Enough to say the app is alive on a slow cold start, quiet enough to sit behind a login form.

Only two properties move

transform and opacity — both composited on the GPU. Nothing here forces a layout pass, which is what keeps it smooth on a cheap Android.

One exception, deliberately

The Paid state animates fill, which is not composited. It fires once on a single element, so the cost is nothing — but do not reach for it in a loop.

Every part needs transform-box

Without transform-box: fill-box an SVG child scales from the viewBox corner, not its own. That single line is the difference between an awning unfurling and the whole mark sliding off.
