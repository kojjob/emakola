The window over featured_rank is derived from today's date, so it is the same shop for every visitor all day and a different one tomorrow. Every featured merchant gets a real turn, the page still caches, and a shopper can go back to the shop they saw this morning.

The rule that saves the page

A thin rail hides itself

A rail that cannot fill at least four cards does not render at all. One lonely shop under a confident heading reads as a broken shop, not a small one — and with twenty shops on the platform most category rails will be empty on day one.

So the page has no fixed number of rails. It shows the ones that are full today and grows more as merchants join, without anyone touching a template.

What this costs to build

Seven of the eight rails run on columns the Store resource already has — featured_rank, view_count, inserted_at, region, the theme, and the product_count aggregate. They are new read actions, not new data.

Only “Selling now” needs anything new: Store has no orders relationship today, so it would take one aggregate counting orders in the last seven days. It is also the strongest signal on the page — a shopper trusts a shop that is selling more than one that is merely new.
