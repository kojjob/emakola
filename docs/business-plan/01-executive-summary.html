West Africa's ecommerce market exceeds $40 billion with less than 5% online retail penetration — the largest untapped ecommerce opportunity on the planet. Over 1.1 billion mobile money accounts across Africa have created a payment infrastructure that bypasses traditional banking entirely. Ghana alone has 24.5 million active mobile money wallets in a population of 34.7 million.

Yet the platforms available to West African merchants are either too basic (Paystack Commerce, Flutterwave Store — essentially payment links with a product page) or too expensive and culturally misaligned (Shopify at $39/mo = GHS 430+ with no native mobile money, no WhatsApp, and pages optimized for broadband, not 3G).

The Problem

What Merchants Need
  • Accept MTN MoMo, Vodafone Cash as primary checkout
  • WhatsApp order notifications (93% penetration in Ghana)
  • Stores that load in <3 seconds on 3G mobile
  • Affordable pricing in local currency (GHS, not USD)
  • Built-in delivery coordination
  • Cash on delivery with proper workflow
What They Get Today
  • Shopify: $39/mo, no mobile money, 15-second loads on 3G
  • Paystack Commerce: Free but no themes, no inventory, no delivery
  • Jumia: 10-25% commission, no customer ownership
  • WhatsApp: Manual order tracking, missed orders, no analytics
  • WooCommerce: Requires developer, hosting, and plugin management
  • Nothing purpose-built for West African realities

The Solution: Emakola

Shopify Built for West Africa

Emakola is a multi-tenant ecommerce platform that enables merchants to create professional online stores with native mobile money payments (MTN MoMo, Vodafone Cash, AirtelTigo), WhatsApp-first communication, a built-in rider delivery marketplace, and storefronts optimized for 3G mobile devices — starting at GHS 49/mo (~$4.50).

$ Mobile Money Native

MTN MoMo, Vodafone Cash, and AirtelTigo are the primary checkout methods — not afterthought plugins. Paystack + Hubtel gateway integration handles cards, bank transfers, and USSD too.

W WhatsApp-First

Order confirmations, shipping updates, abandoned cart recovery, and customer support — all via WhatsApp. Because 91.8% of Ghanaian internet users are on WhatsApp, not checking email.

3G 3G-Optimized

Built with Elixir/Phoenix LiveView — server-rendered with ~30KB JS payload vs 300KB+ React SPAs. Pages load in <3 seconds on typical Ghanaian mobile connections.

D Built-in Delivery

WhatsApp-based rider marketplace connects merchants with local delivery riders. Zone-based pricing, GPS tracking, COD with driver confirmation. No separate logistics app needed.

Why Now

1 Mobile Money Interoperability

Ghana launched full cross-network mobile money interoperability in 2021 — customers can pay from any network. Transaction values doubled to GHS 3+ trillion in 2024.

2 Post-COVID Digital Adoption

COVID permanently accelerated digital adoption. Online spending grew 40%+ during 2020-2021, and the behavior stuck. Merchants who went digital aren't going back.

3 No Dominant Platform

There is no Shopify for West Africa. Paystack Commerce is too basic. Shopify is too expensive. The market leader position is open — and first-mover advantage compounds.

4 Smartphone Price Collapse

Chinese brands (Tecno, Infinix, Itel) brought smartphones under $50. With 38M+ mobile connections and 4G expanding, the infrastructure is ready.

Impact

Emakola isn't just commercially viable — it's structurally impactful. Every merchant onboarded is a small business formalized. Every transaction is financial inclusion. Every rider connected is a job created. With 44% of Ghana's SMEs women-owned, and our primary persona being women Instagram sellers, we target 60%+ women-led businesses on the platform. Aligned with SDGs 1, 5, 8, 9, and 10.

West Africa doesn't need another marketplace taking 20% from merchants. It doesn't need a Silicon Valley platform priced in dollars. It needs infrastructure — built locally, priced locally, designed for mobile money and WhatsApp and 3G. Emakola is that infrastructure. The Emakola Team
