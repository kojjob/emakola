Organizational Structure

Emakola's team scales across three distinct phases, each aligned to company milestones and revenue targets. The structure stays lean and engineering-heavy in early phases, expanding into customer success and growth functions as the merchant base grows.

Phase 1: Launch (0-6 Months) — 3-4 People

Phase 2: Growth (6-18 Months) — 8-10 People

Phase 3: Scale (18-36 Months) — 15-20 People

Compensation Advantage

A senior Elixir engineer in Accra costs GHS 8,000-15,000/mo ($516-$968) — compared to $8,000-15,000/mo in the US or EU. This 10x cost advantage allows Emakola to build a world-class engineering team at a fraction of Silicon Valley rates, while paying competitive local salaries that attract top Ghanaian talent.

Operational Processes

Merchant Onboarding

Signup to Live Store in Under 10 Minutes
1. Sign Up Email + Phone Number
2. Verify Phone SMS OTP Verification
3. Create Store 3-Step Wizard (Name, Category, Theme)
4. Add First Product Photo + Price + Description
5. Connect Payment Paystack or Mobile Money
6. Go Live Store Published

Target Metrics

  • Signup to live store: Under 10 minutes
  • First sale: Within 7 days of going live
  • Activation rate: 60%+ complete onboarding

Design Principles

  • Mobile-first — works entirely on a phone
  • Minimal required fields — no friction
  • Progress indicator at every step
  • WhatsApp welcome message on completion

Customer Support Strategy

Support Channels
  • Primary: WhatsApp — matches how Ghanaian merchants already communicate with customers
  • Secondary: In-app live chat — for merchants already in the dashboard
  • Tertiary: Email — for formal issues, billing, documentation

Response Time Targets

  • In-app chat: < 1 hour (business hours)

Infrastructure Philosophy

Elixir/Phoenix's exceptional concurrency model (powered by the BEAM VM) means Emakola can handle thousands of concurrent connections on modest hardware. A single Fly.io machine can serve hundreds of active storefronts — keeping infrastructure costs remarkably low during the growth phase.

Vendor Dependencies & Risk Mitigation

Every external dependency is evaluated for business risk and paired with a concrete mitigation strategy. The architecture follows a "gateway behaviour" pattern — all vendor integrations sit behind abstraction layers, enabling rapid switching.

Vendor Service Risk Level Mitigation Strategy
Paystack Card + Mobile Money payments High Hubtel as backup gateway; abstract behind Gateway behaviour for instant switching
Hubtel Mobile Money payments High Paystack as backup; dual-gateway architecture already in place
Fly.io Application hosting Medium Fully Dockerized; portable to any cloud provider (AWS, GCP, Hetzner) within hours
WhatsApp (Meta) Business API notifications Medium SMS fallback always available; notification worker supports multiple channels
AWS / DigitalOcean S3-compatible file storage Low S3-compatible API; easy migration between any S3-compatible provider
Postmark Transactional email Low SMTP-based; can switch to any email provider (SES, Mailgun) with config change
