Operating break-even

Monthly operating break-even is reached by Month 4 (85 merchants) due to the low cost base. However, cumulative cash break-even — accounting for all pre-launch and ramp-up costs funded by the seed round — occurs at Month 16, when cumulative revenue exceeds cumulative costs plus the initial investment drawdown.

Cash Flow Projection

The J-Curve: Cash Flow Trajectory

Months 1–3 represent the deepest cash burn as team costs and marketing run ahead of revenue. With GHS 775,000 in seed funding, the cash position reaches its minimum at approximately Month 3 (GHS ~735,000 remaining) before revenue inflection begins in Month 4.

By Month 4, monthly operating cash flow turns positive. The cumulative cash position recovers through Months 4–15, crossing back to the original seed amount by Month 16 (cumulative break-even). From Month 16 onward, the business is self-funding with growing cash reserves.

Under the conservative scenario (700 merchants, 7% churn), break-even extends to Month 22 — still within the 24-month outer runway provided by the seed round plus retained operating revenue from Month 4 onward.

The J-curve is shallow by SaaS standards: peak cash consumption is only GHS 40,000 ($2,580) in the worst month, and the business generates positive monthly operating cash flow within 4 months. This capital efficiency is driven by Accra-based team costs (80% lower than US equivalents), lean infrastructure on Fly.io, and high-margin transaction fee revenue that scales without proportional cost increases.
