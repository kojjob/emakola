The one idea behind everything

Like the market queen who shares the money on the spot — but done by the bank, every time.

In an old market, the trusted konkohemaa (market queen) could collect a payment and hand each person their fair share immediately. Emakola does the same thing — but the bank does the sharing automatically, so it is fair, instant, and impossible to cheat.

The old way (risky)

The customer's money lands in one person's pocket. Everyone else must wait and trust that person to pay them later. Sometimes that person disappears with the money.

The Emakola way (safe)

The moment the customer pays, the bank splits the money and sends each person their share directly. No waiting. No trusting a stranger. No one can run away with what isn't theirs.

Why it matters

A trader with no money can now sell. A wholesaler is paid before shipping. A buyer pays safely. Everybody wins, and nobody depends on anybody's honesty.

Plain-words dictionary

Big words, made simple.

MoMo
Mobile Money — paying with the wallet on your phone (MTN, Telecel, AirtelTigo). No bank account needed.
Dropshipping
Selling goods you don't own or store. The wholesaler keeps the stock and ships it for you. You only sell.
Wholesaler / Supplier
The person who owns the goods in bulk, sets the cost price, and ships orders to buyers.
Split payment
One payment that is divided and sent to several people at the same moment.
Subaccount
Your own pocket at the bank where your share of every sale lands directly.
Settlement
When the money actually arrives in your MoMo or bank account.
Margin / Profit
What the trader keeps: the selling price minus the wholesaler's cost (and a small Emakola fee).
