What actually matters in Ghana

Before comparing names, fix the criteria. For a store selling to Ghanaian buyers, the platform must:

  • Accept mobile money natively. MTN MoMo, Telecel Cash, and AT Money are how your customers pay. If MoMo requires a plugin, a workaround, or "coming soon," everything else is irrelevant.
  • Charge in cedis. Dollar subscriptions mean your costs rise every time the exchange rate moves — before you have sold anything.
  • Load fast on a mid-range phone. Your buyers are on mobile data, often 3G/4G on an Infinix or Tecno — not fibre on a MacBook. A beautiful theme that takes ten seconds to load is a closed door.
  • Speak WhatsApp. Order confirmations that arrive by WhatsApp/SMS get read. Email in Ghana is where notifications go to be ignored.

The contenders

Shopify

The world's best-known store builder, and genuinely excellent software — for its home market. From Ghana, the friction stacks up. Your subscription is billed in US dollars — Shopify only prices plans in a short list of local currencies, and the cedi is not on it. Shopify Payments does not operate in Ghana at all, so mobile money can only reach your checkout through a local gateway you contract yourself. That part works well — Paystack is Shopify's own preferred provider for Ghana and does support MoMo — but because it is a third-party gateway, Shopify charges its own transaction fee on top of the gateway's fee, on every order, with no Ghanaian route out of it. Shopify makes sense for a Ghanaian brand selling primarily to the US/UK in dollars. Selling to Ghanaians — you would be paying international prices to fight the platform on the one feature that matters most.

WooCommerce (WordPress)

Free software, full control — and you become the IT department. You buy hosting, install plugins, patch security updates, and debug the checkout the night it breaks. MoMo works through local payment plugins, but every layer (hosting, WordPress, WooCommerce, theme, payment plugin) is another thing only you are responsible for. A fine choice if you have a developer; a heavy one if you are a merchant.

Marketplaces: Jiji, Tonaton

Listing sites give you traffic, and for second-hand goods and price-shoppers they are useful. Worth knowing they are one landlord, not two: Jiji acquired Tonaton in 2022, and both sites run today under the same Ghanaian company. But you own nothing on either: no brand, no customer list, no repeat-purchase relationship — and buyers still arrange payment however they and the seller agree, with all the screenshot problems that carries. Marketplaces are a channel, not a home.

Instagram/WhatsApp only

Zero platform cost, maximum labour cost. You are the catalogue, the checkout, and the payment verification. We wrote a whole piece on this: selling on WhatsApp without chasing screenshots. Social selling is powerful — as the front door, not the whole shop.

How to actually decide

  1. Selling to Ghanaians in cedis? Choose the platform where MoMo is native, not an add-on. This is the whole game.
  2. Selling abroad in dollars? Shopify earns its cost — international payments and apps are its home turf.
  3. Have a developer on the team? WooCommerce buys flexibility with maintenance hours.
  4. Not sure yet? Start where starting is free and takes an afternoon. You learn more from ten real orders than from ten comparison articles — and if you outgrow the choice, your product photos and customer list travel with you.

Common questions

Is Shopify available in Ghana?

Yes, you can sign up from Ghana — the constraints are practical, not legal. Your bill comes in US dollars, Shopify Payments is unavailable here, and the gateway you must use to take MoMo carries Shopify's third-party transaction fee on top. It fits dollar-earning export brands far better than cedi-earning local ones.

What is the cheapest way to start selling online in Ghana?

A free store on a platform with no monthly fee. Makola's Starter plan costs nothing until you sell — the true minimum-risk start. Read our complete starting guide for the full checklist.

Can I accept MoMo payments on any of these platforms?

Eventually, on most — the difference is native versus bolted-on. Built-in MoMo means payment confirmations, order matching, and receipts work as one system. Bolted-on means you maintain the seams. Our MoMo payments guide goes deeper.

Do I need my own .com domain to look serious?

Not to start. A clean store link with your business name, real photos, and working checkout builds more trust than a bare domain with none of those. Add a custom domain when the brand earns it.
